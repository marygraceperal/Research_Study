1
START

Identify the Classroom Problem

Classroom observation: Students experienced difficulty solving financial and ordinary annuity problems using mathematical formulas, while some could obtain answers through spreadsheets.

The central research curiosity is not simply whether students can obtain a correct answer. The deeper question is whether a spreadsheet-generated answer reflects genuine mathematical understanding.

Important methodological rule

Informal experiences and previous classroom observations may motivate the research problem, but they must not automatically be treated as formal research data.

Formal research data begin with the approved research procedure and formal data-collection point.

2
FRAME

Define the Research Problem and Gap

Research problem

Students may experience difficulty with formula-based ordinary annuity problem solving, while spreadsheet tools may make numerical calculation easier.

Research gap

The unresolved classroom uncertainty is whether differences between formula-based and spreadsheet-based approaches are associated with differences in problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding.

Key idea: Getting the answer is not necessarily the same as understanding the mathematics.
3
QUESTIONS

Research Questions and Hypotheses

Main Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Specific Questions

  1. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding before the instructional intervention?
  2. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following formula-based instruction?
  3. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following spreadsheet-based instruction?
  4. Is there a significant difference in problem-solving performance between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction?
  5. Is there a significant difference in conceptual understanding between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction?
  6. What instructional implications can be drawn from the findings?

Hypotheses

Outcome Null Hypothesis Alternative Hypothesis
PSP There is no significant difference in students’ problem-solving performance between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction. There is a significant difference.
CU There is no significant difference in students’ conceptual understanding between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction. There is a significant difference.
Do not assume before analysis that spreadsheet-based instruction will be better. The research should test the difference.
4
DESIGN

Research Design

Recommended Design

Classroom-Based Action Research using a Counterbalanced Within-Subject Design

Every learner experiences both instructional approaches. The order is counterbalanced so that some learners experience formula-based instruction first and others experience spreadsheet-based instruction first.

Group Planning N* First Instruction First Form Second Instruction Second Form
G1 9 Formula A Spreadsheet B
G2 9 Formula B Spreadsheet A
G3 8 Spreadsheet A Formula B
G4 8 Spreadsheet B Formula A
Planning assumption: 34 learners and the 9/9/8/8 allocation are planning assumptions. Actual allocation must follow the approved procedure and documented random assignment.

Important Limitation

Because each learner receives both conditions, carryover and practice effects remain possible. This should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings.

CONTENT

Financial Mathematics Content

Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity

FV = PMT × [(1 + i)n − 1] / i

Used to determine the accumulated value of equal periodic payments made at the end of each period.

Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + i)−n] / i

Used to determine the present value supported by a series of future periodic payments.

Required Periodic Payment

PMT = PV × i / [1 − (1 + i)−n]

Critical Rate and Period Relationship

i = r / m      n = mt

The annual interest rate must correspond to the number of compounding periods, and the number of periods must correspond to the payment/ compounding schedule.

Partial-Credit Rules

  • Correct setup with arithmetic error retains substantial credit.
  • Do not double-penalize the same downstream rate-conversion error.
  • Wrong formula loses model points, but consistent later work may earn partial credit.
  • A final answer without required reasoning receives only evidence-supported points.
  • Blank responses receive zero.
  • Carry-forward errors are not repeatedly penalized.

ETHICS

Permission, Ethics, and Data Protection

Required safeguards

Teacher–Researcher Dual Role

Participation, assessment, and research procedures should not create unfair academic consequences. The researcher should avoid coercion, protect confidentiality, and separate research records from unnecessary identifying information.

Do not invent official school/division forms. Use the current requirements communicated by the authorized office.

Four-Point Rating

  1. Not Appropriate
  2. Needs Major Revision
  3. Appropriate with Minor Revision
  4. Highly Appropriate
I-CVI = Number of Experts Rating 3 or 4 / Total Number of Experts

For five experts, an I-CVI of 1.00 is strong; .80 is generally acceptable; lower values require review. Comments and technical errors override mechanical reliance on the numeric value.

Validity is not the same as reliability. A later pilot/reliability process may be required.

RANDOMIZATION

Random Assignment and Counterbalancing

Procedure

  1. Assign each eligible learner a research ID.
  2. Generate random values.
  3. Sort the random values.
  4. Assign learners sequentially to the planned groups.
  5. Record the allocation.
  6. Do not rerandomize because of outcome data.
  7. Document withdrawals or changes in the status log.
The randomization record must be created before outcome analysis.

Required Audit Trail

  • Research ID
  • Random value
  • Assigned group
  • Sequence
  • Form allocation
  • Status
  • Date/time of change
  • Reason for documented change
12
PRETEST

Formal Pretest

The formal pretest establishes the baseline before the counterbalanced instructional conditions.

Administration Rule

  • Use Form P.
  • Use the same fixed testing time for all learners.
  • Recommended maximum: 60 minutes.
  • Record attendance and administration conditions.
  • Do not fabricate missing responses.
  • Score according to the approved scoring guide.
The pretest is primarily descriptive baseline evidence. It should not automatically be treated as a covariate unless the analytical design explicitly supports that decision.

Descriptive Statistics

  • N
  • Mean

  • Minimum

Paired Difference

Di = Spreadsheeti − Formulai

Primary Statistical Test

Use a paired-samples t-test when the paired-difference assumptions are reasonably satisfied.

If assumptions are not reasonably satisfied, use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Effect Size

Cohen's dz = Mean Difference / SD of Paired Differences

For a Wilcoxon analysis, report an appropriate matched-pairs rank-biserial effect size.

Additional Diagnostics

  • Formula-first vs spreadsheet-first groups
  • Period 1 vs Period 2
  • Form A vs Form B
  • Fidelity/deviation records

Assessment Forms P / A / B

Form P — Pretest

Baseline assessment before the formal instructional conditions. Use the approved Form P item set and scoring guide.

Form A — Condition Assessment

Parallel assessment form covering FV, PV, required periodic payment, conceptual understanding, problem solving, and spreadsheet–mathematics reasoning.

Form B — Parallel Condition Assessment

Parallel assessment form matched to Form A in construct coverage, difficulty intention, scoring structure, and cognitive demand.

Administration time: 60 minutes maximum per assessment. Do not administer two full assessment forms as a single testing session.

Replication and Reuse

Three Layers

Layer 1 — Research Protocol

Stable reusable methodology, instruments, scoring rules, and procedures.

Layer 2 — Learner Cohort

A newly established group of eligible learners for each implementation.

Layer 3 — Research Evidence

Replication does not automatically establish generalizability. Differences in learners, setting, teacher, technology, and implementation must be documented.

Researcher Reminder

Do not let the technology become the research outcome.

The spreadsheet is an instructional approach. The primary question is what happens to students' mathematical problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding under the two instructional conditions.

Research principle:

A correct spreadsheet output is evidence of a numerical result. It is not, by itself, sufficient evidence of conceptual understanding.

Final research logic:
