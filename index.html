Classroom-Based Action Research Counterbalanced Within-Subject Design Grade 11 General Mathematics Ordinary Annuities

Worked Exemplar:
Comparing Formula-Based and Spreadsheet-Based Instruction in Students’ Problem-Solving Performance and Conceptual Understanding of Ordinary Annuities

Overview Design Assessment 14 Steps Templates Analysis Replication Audit

1. The Research We Actually Planned

Classroom Observation: Students experienced difficulty solving financial/ordinary annuity problems using formulas, while they appeared able to obtain answers using spreadsheets.

Research Problem

The classroom concern is not simply whether students can obtain a numerical answer. The deeper concern is whether the method used supports genuine mathematical problem-solving and conceptual understanding.

Research Gap

Does obtaining a correct spreadsheet answer demonstrate genuine conceptual understanding and mathematical reasoning?

Central Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Evidence Boundary: Earlier informal classroom experiences motivated this research but are not automatically formal research data. Formal research evidence begins with the approved research procedure and actual data collection.

2. Research Logic

Observation → Problem → Gap → Research Questions → Variables → Design → TOS → Instruments → Validation → Ethics → Randomization → Pretest → Intervention → Assessment → Data → Analysis → Reflection → Action Plan
Action Research Cycle: PLAN → ACT → OBSERVE → ANALYZE → REFLECT → IMPROVE

3. Research Design

Research Type

Classroom-Based Action Research

Design

Counterbalanced Within-Subject / Repeated-Measures Design

Primary Outcomes

Problem-Solving Performance (PSP)
Conceptual Understanding (CU)

Supporting Evidence

Spreadsheet–Mathematics Reasoning (SMR)

Counterbalanced Sequence

Group Planning N* First Instruction First Form Second Instruction Second Form
G1 9 Formula A Spreadsheet B
G2 9 Formula B Spreadsheet A
G3 8 Spreadsheet A Formula B
G4 8 Spreadsheet B Formula A

*34 learners and the 9/9/8/8 allocation are planning assumptions. Actual allocation must follow eligibility, approval, and documented randomization.

Why Within-Subject?

Every learner experiences both instructional approaches, allowing the learner's performance under one condition to be compared with the same learner's performance under the other.

Why Counterbalance?

It balances Formula-first and Spreadsheet-first instructional order and balances Form A/B exposure.

Remaining Limitation

Carryover, practice, period, and possible form effects cannot be assumed to disappear. They must be documented as limitations and examined descriptively.

5. Financial Mathematics Content

Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity

FV = PMT [ ((1+i)n − 1) / i ]

Used to calculate the total accumulated value of equal end-of-period payments.

Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

PV = PMT [ (1 − (1+i)−n) / i ]

Used to calculate the current value of equal future payments, such as the principal of a loan.

Required Periodic Payment

PMT = PV [ i / (1 − (1+i)−n) ]

Used to determine the periodic payment required to settle a loan over the stated number of periods.

Critical Period-Matching Rule: The annual interest rate must be converted to the interest rate per payment/compounding period, and the time must be converted to the corresponding number of periods.

Correct setup + arithmetic error Retain substantial credit when the rubric evidence supports it.
Repeated rate-conversion error Do not double-penalize the same underlying error through every downstream criterion.
Wrong formula Model points may be lost, but consistent subsequent work may still receive appropriate partial credit.
Carry-forward error Do not repeatedly penalize the same inherited error.
Blank 0.
Final answer only Award only the evidence-supported points when the task requires a complete solution.

9. Spreadsheet-Based Instruction Safeguards

Required Mathematical Reasoning

  1. Understand the financial situation.
  2. Identify mathematical variables.
  3. Determine payment frequency.
  4. Determine periodic interest rate.
  5. Determine number of periods.
  6. Select FV, PV, or PMT.
  7. Map mathematical variables into the spreadsheet.
  8. Generate the result.
  9. Check reasonableness.
  10. Interpret the financial meaning.

11. The Complete 14-Step Research Procedure

01 02 03 04 05 06 07 08 09 10 11 12 13 14
01
STEP 01

Classroom Observation & Context

Purpose: Start with a real classroom learning concern.

Procedure

  1. Record what learners were actually doing.
  2. Identify the mathematical topic involved.
  3. Document the learning difficulty.
  4. Separate observation from interpretation.
  5. Explain why the concern matters.

🟡 Planning Assumption

The exemplar began from observed difficulty with ordinary-annuity formulas and apparent spreadsheet success.

Important Reminder

Earlier informal classroom experiences do not automatically become formal research data.

Completion Evidence

Observation notes, work samples, diagnostic evidence, or other actual classroom records.

02
STEP 02

Research Problem & Research Gap

Purpose: Turn the observation into an answerable research problem.

Procedure

  1. State the classroom problem precisely.
  2. Identify what remains uncertain.
  3. Explain why the uncertainty matters.
  4. Write the classroom-specific research gap.

🟡 Exemplar Gap

Does obtaining a correct spreadsheet answer demonstrate genuine conceptual understanding?

Important Reminder

A research gap does not necessarily mean that nobody has ever studied the topic. A specific unanswered classroom instructional question can be a valid action-research gap.

03
STEP 03

Research Questions & Hypotheses

Main Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Specific Questions

  1. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding of ordinary annuities before the instructional intervention?
  2. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following formula-based instruction?
  3. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following spreadsheet-based instruction?
  4. Is there a significant difference in students' problem-solving performance between the two instructional approaches?
  5. Is there a significant difference in students' conceptual understanding between the two instructional approaches?
  6. What instructional implications can be drawn from the findings?
Do not assume spreadsheet instruction is better. The study is designed to determine the observed difference rather than predetermine the winner.

STEP 05

Research Design

Use the counterbalanced within-subject design described earlier.

Do not assign learners based on achievement, gender, seating position, teacher preference, or expected performance.
Design principle: Every learner receives both instructional approaches, while instructional order and Form A/B order are counterbalanced.
