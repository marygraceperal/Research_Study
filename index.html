Classroom-Based Action Research Counterbalanced Within-Subject Design Grade 11 General Mathematics Ordinary Annuities

Worked Exemplar:
Comparing Formula-Based and Spreadsheet-Based Instruction in Students’ Problem-Solving Performance and Conceptual Understanding of Ordinary Annuities

Overview Design Assessment 14 Steps Templates Analysis Replication Audit

1. The Research We Actually Planned

Classroom Observation: Students experienced difficulty solving financial/ordinary annuity problems using formulas, while they appeared able to obtain answers using spreadsheets.

Research Problem

The classroom concern is not simply whether students can obtain a numerical answer. The deeper concern is whether the method used supports genuine mathematical problem-solving and conceptual understanding.

Research Gap

Does obtaining a correct spreadsheet answer demonstrate genuine conceptual understanding and mathematical reasoning?

Central Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Evidence Boundary: Earlier informal classroom experiences motivated this research but are not automatically formal research data. Formal research evidence begins with the approved research procedure and actual data collection.

2. Research Logic

Observation → Problem → Gap → Research Questions → Variables → Design → TOS → Instruments → Validation → Ethics → Randomization → Pretest → Intervention → Assessment → Data → Analysis → Reflection → Action Plan
Action Research Cycle: PLAN → ACT → OBSERVE → ANALYZE → REFLECT → IMPROVE

*34 learners and the 9/9/8/8 allocation are planning assumptions. Actual allocation must follow eligibility, approval, and documented randomization.

Why Within-Subject?

Every learner experiences both instructional approaches, allowing the learner's performance under one condition to be compared with the same learner's performance under the other.

Why Counterbalance?

It balances Formula-first and Spreadsheet-first instructional order and balances Form A/B exposure.

Remaining Limitation

Carryover, practice, period, and possible form effects cannot be assumed to disappear. They must be documented as limitations and examined descriptively.

5. Financial Mathematics Content

Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity

FV = PMT [ ((1+i)n − 1) / i ]

Used to calculate the total accumulated value of equal end-of-period payments.

Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

PV = PMT [ (1 − (1+i)−n) / i ]

Used to calculate the current value of equal future payments, such as the principal of a loan.

Required Periodic Payment

PMT = PV [ i / (1 − (1+i)−n) ]

Used to determine the periodic payment required to settle a loan over the stated number of periods.

Critical Period-Matching Rule: The annual interest rate must be converted to the interest rate per payment/compounding period, and the time must be converted to the corresponding number of periods.

11. The Complete 14-Step Research Procedure

01
STEP 01

Classroom Observation & Context

Purpose: Start with a real classroom learning concern.

Procedure

  1. Record what learners were actually doing.
  2. Identify the mathematical topic involved.
  3. Document the learning difficulty.
  4. Separate observation from interpretation.
  5. Explain why the concern matters.

🟡 Planning Assumption

The exemplar began from observed difficulty with ordinary-annuity formulas and apparent spreadsheet success.

Important Reminder

Earlier informal classroom experiences do not automatically become formal research data.

Completion Evidence

Observation notes, work samples, diagnostic evidence, or other actual classroom records.

02
STEP 02

Research Problem & Research Gap

Purpose: Turn the observation into an answerable research problem.

Procedure

  1. State the classroom problem precisely.
  2. Identify what remains uncertain.
  3. Explain why the uncertainty matters.
  4. Write the classroom-specific research gap.

🟡 Exemplar Gap

Does obtaining a correct spreadsheet answer demonstrate genuine conceptual understanding?

Important Reminder

A research gap does not necessarily mean that nobody has ever studied the topic. A specific unanswered classroom instructional question can be a valid action-research gap.

03
STEP 03

Research Questions & Hypotheses

Main Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Specific Questions

  1. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding of ordinary annuities before the instructional intervention?
  2. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following formula-based instruction?
  3. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following spreadsheet-based instruction?
  4. Is there a significant difference in students' problem-solving performance between the two instructional approaches?
  5. Is there a significant difference in students' conceptual understanding between the two instructional approaches?
  6. What instructional implications can be drawn from the findings?
Do not assume spreadsheet instruction is better. The study is designed to determine the observed difference rather than predetermine the winner.

9.3 Teacher-Researcher Dual-Role Safeguards

Same Learning Opportunity

All learners receive the same curriculum content and comparable instructional opportunities.

Neutrality

The teacher-researcher should not communicate that one instructional approach is expected to be superior.

Confidentiality

Research IDs should be used in research files rather than learner names whenever practical.

No Research Penalty

Participation decisions should not unfairly affect classroom grades or access to learning.

9.4 Data Protection Procedure

  1. Assign each learner a research ID.
  2. Store the ID-to-name key separately from research datasets.
  3. Use research IDs in spreadsheets and analysis files.
  4. Restrict access to research records.
  5. Do not include unnecessary personal information in analysis files.
  6. Do not publish identifiable learner information.
  7. Maintain a record of data access and corrections when appropriate.
  8. Follow applicable school/division requirements for retention and disposal.

10.4 Item-Level Content Validity Index

I-CVI = Number of experts rating the item 3 or 4 Number of experts

With five experts, for example:

  • 1.00 — strong evidence of agreement
  • 0.80 — generally acceptable
  • 0.60 — weak; review recommended
  • 0.40 or lower — poor agreement; major review needed
Expert judgment overrides blind numerical acceptance. A mathematically incorrect item must be corrected even if its numerical CVI happens to appear acceptable.

10.5 Important Distinction

Validity

Concerns whether the instrument appropriately represents what it is intended to measure.

Reliability

Concerns consistency of measurement. Reliability should not be claimed merely because experts rated the instrument.

13.10 Carryover / Practice Effect

Because each learner receives both instructional conditions, carryover and practice effects cannot be completely eliminated. Counterbalancing reduces, but does not guarantee elimination of, order effects.

Therefore, conclusions should describe observed differences rather than claiming that one approach universally causes superior learning.

EVIDENCE → ACTION

Analysis, Reflection & Action Plan

Convert the collected evidence into defensible findings, teacher reflection and an evidence-based improvement plan.

14.1 Primary Research Outcomes

Problem-Solving Performance

Primary outcome measuring students' ability to understand a financial problem, identify information and variables, select an appropriate mathematical model, perform calculations and interpret the result.

Conceptual Understanding

Primary outcome measuring students' understanding of meanings, relationships, timing, variables, rates, formulas and financial interpretation.

Spreadsheet–Mathematics Reasoning

Supporting evidence concerning the learner's ability to connect spreadsheet inputs and outputs to mathematical reasoning.

14.2 Score Ceilings

Outcome Maximum Percentage

PSP 105 PSP ÷ 105 × 100
SMR 12 SMR ÷ 12 × 100
Do not use the combined 133-point score as the primary research outcome. CU, PSP and SMR represent different constructs and should remain analytically distinct.

14.3 Descriptive Statistics

For each condition and primary outcome, calculate:

  • N
  • Mean
  • Median
  • Standard deviation
  • Minimum
  • Maximum

14.4 Within-Learner Difference

Di = Spreadsheeti − Formulai

Calculate the difference separately for PSP and CU.

A positive mean difference indicates that the spreadsheet condition produced a higher observed score in the dataset; it does not by itself prove causation.

14.5 Primary Statistical Test

Paired-Samples t-Test

Use when the paired differences are reasonably suitable for the assumptions of the test.

Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test

Use as the nonparametric alternative when the assumptions for the paired t-test are not reasonably met.

14.6 Effect Size

dz = Mean paired difference SD of paired differences

For a Wilcoxon analysis, a matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation may be reported as an effect-size measure.

14.7 Confidence Interval

Where appropriate, report a confidence interval for the mean paired difference to communicate the precision of the observed estimate.

14.8 Pretest Analysis

Form P should primarily provide a descriptive baseline. It should not automatically be inserted as a covariate simply because it exists.

If baseline imbalance or other issues require a more complex analysis, the researcher should document the methodological reason before changing the analysis plan.

14.9 Sequence / Order Diagnostic

Compare results descriptively between:

  • Formula-first learners
  • Spreadsheet-first learners
  • Period 1
  • Period 2
  • Form A
  • Form B

These checks help identify possible order, practice or form effects. Avoid constructing a complicated statistical model unless the dataset and research question justify it.

14.10 Interpretation Scenarios

A — Spreadsheet Higher in PSP and CU

The spreadsheet condition shows higher observed performance in both primary outcomes. The finding may suggest that the approach was associated with stronger performance under the study conditions.

B — Spreadsheet Higher PSP, Similar CU

Learners may have demonstrated more efficient problem-solving without a clear difference in conceptual understanding.

C — Spreadsheet Higher PSP, Lower CU

The finding may indicate that learners can obtain computational results efficiently without demonstrating equivalent conceptual understanding.

D — Formula Higher PSP and CU

The observed data may favor formula-based instruction under the particular study conditions.

E — No Meaningful Difference

The two instructional conditions may have produced similar observed outcomes under the implementation conditions.

Never decide the interpretation before seeing the data. These scenarios are guides for interpreting possible findings, not predetermined conclusions.

RESEARCH LOGIC

Research Questions & Hypotheses

Main Research Question

How do formula-based and spreadsheet-based instructional approaches differ in developing students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding in financial applications of ordinary annuities?

Specific Questions

  1. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding of ordinary annuities before the instructional intervention?
  2. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following formula-based instruction?
  3. What is the level of students' problem-solving performance following spreadsheet-based instruction?
  4. Is there a significant difference in students' problem-solving performance between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction?
  5. Is there a significant difference in students' conceptual understanding between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction?
  6. What instructional implications can be drawn from the findings for teaching ordinary annuities?

Hypotheses

H₀₁: There is no significant difference in students' problem-solving performance between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction in solving ordinary annuity problems.
H₁₁: There is a significant difference in students' problem-solving performance between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction.
H₀₂: There is no significant difference in students' conceptual understanding between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction in ordinary annuities.
H₁₂: There is a significant difference in students' conceptual understanding between formula-based and spreadsheet-based instruction.
Do not assume that spreadsheet-based instruction is better. The research must remain open to either direction of the findings.

REUSE & REPLICATION

Reusable Research Protocol

The system is designed to be reusable. However, reuse does not mean that the next implementation automatically reproduces the same participants, scores or findings.

Three-Layer Model

Layer 1 — Research Protocol

Stable methodological architecture: research questions, constructs, instruments, procedures, scoring and analysis rules.

Layer 2 — Learner Cohort

Re-established for each implementation: participants, research IDs, attendance, random assignment and contextual conditions.

Layer 3 — Research Evidence

Newly generated data, scores, fidelity records, analysis and findings.

Stable Elements

  • Research problem logic
  • Research questions
  • Construct definitions
  • Assessment blueprint
  • Scoring rules
  • Instructional parallelism
  • Counterbalanced structure
  • Analysis framework
  • Reflection framework

Elements Recreated Each Implementation

  • Participant list
  • Research IDs
  • Random assignment
  • Attendance
  • Actual scores
  • Fidelity records
  • Deviations
  • Statistical results
  • Reflection
  • Action plan
Replication is not automatic generalization. A successful replication provides additional evidence under another implementation context; it does not automatically establish universal effectiveness.

Replication Decision Rule

Before declaring a replication successful, verify:

  1. The same core research questions were retained.
  2. The same constructs were measured.
  3. The instrument versions are documented.
  4. The implementation procedure was sufficiently comparable.
  5. Any deviations were documented.
  6. The analysis was conducted according to the planned rules.
  7. Differences in context are explicitly reported.

NON-NEGOTIABLE

Research Integrity Rules

01 — Never fabricate data.

Planning assumptions may guide implementation, but they cannot be presented as actual learner results.

02 — Never fabricate approvals.

An approval template is not evidence that approval has been granted.

03 — Never change the analysis because the result is inconvenient.

Any legitimate methodological change must have a documented reason.

04 — Preserve the original data.

Cleaning should create a documented clean dataset rather than silently replacing the original.

05 — Separate constructs.

CU, PSP and SMR should not be collapsed into a single primary score.

06 — Do not overclaim causation.

Interpret findings within the actual classroom conditions and acknowledge carryover, order and implementation limitations.

07 — Document deviations.

A deviation is not automatically a failure; undocumented deviation is the bigger methodological problem.

08 — Protect learner identity.

Use research IDs and avoid unnecessary personally identifying information.
