My Complete Research Task Roadmap

Study: Comparing Formula-Based and Spreadsheet-Based Instruction in Students' Problem-Solving Performance and Conceptual Understanding of Ordinary Annuities

A practical roadmap from identifying the classroom problem to implementation, analysis, reflection, final reporting, archiving, and replication.

🎯 The Heart of the Research

Main research question:

Do students demonstrate different levels of problem-solving performance and conceptual understanding when ordinary annuities are taught through formula-based instruction versus spreadsheet-based instruction?

Central idea: A student getting the correct answer from a spreadsheet does not automatically mean the student understands the mathematics behind the answer.

🛡️ PHASE 2 — Secure Research Clearance

Because you are both teacher and researcher, protect learners and follow the applicable school/division/research-office requirements.

  • ☐ School/research-office permission as required
  • ☐ Parent/guardian consent
  • ☐ Learner assent
  • ☐ Participant information
  • ☐ Confidentiality procedures
  • ☐ Research IDs rather than names in the analytical dataset
  • ☐ Researcher declaration
  • ☐ Teacher-researcher dual-role safeguards
  • ☐ Data-protection procedure
Data boundary: Earlier informal classroom experiences may explain why the problem was noticed, but they should not be presented as formal research data unless collected under the approved protocol.

📐 PHASE 3 — Validate the Instruments

Experts review Form P, Form A, and Form B for:

  • Relevance
  • Accuracy
  • Clarity
  • Grade-level appropriateness
  • Alignment
  • Assessment suitability
I-CVI = Number of experts rating 3 or 4 ÷ Total number of experts

Consider numerical validity evidence together with expert comments and mathematical/construct review. A mathematical error or serious construct mismatch must be corrected even if a numerical validity index looks acceptable.

🔐 PHASE 4 — Final Instrument Release Gate

  • ☐ FV, PV, and PMT mathematics independently checked
  • ☐ Periodic rate and number-of-period calculations checked
  • ☐ Numerical answers verified
  • ☐ PSP/CU/SMR construct mapping checked
  • ☐ TOS and rubrics aligned
  • ☐ Form A/B equivalence reviewed
  • ☐ Spreadsheet tasks assess mathematical reasoning rather than generic computer skill
  • ☐ Administration time standardized at 60 minutes
  • ☐ Ethics/permission gates completed
  • ☐ Version number locked before release

Release rule: Do not release the final instrument while a critical gate remains unresolved.

👩‍🏫 PHASE 7 — Teach the First Condition

Formula-Based Instruction

Understand → Identify → Convert rate → Determine periods → Select formula → Substitute → Calculate → Check → Interpret

Spreadsheet-Based Instruction

Understand → Identify → Convert rate → Determine periods → Map variables → Set up spreadsheet → Calculate → Check → Interpret

⚖️ PHASE 8 — Keep the Two Conditions Fair

The two conditions should have the same:

  • Learning objectives
  • Mathematical content
  • Examples or comparable examples
  • Difficulty
  • Practice opportunities
  • Teacher
  • Classroom
  • Instructional duration
  • Assessment targets
Key principle: The main planned difference should be the instructional/computational approach— not extra time, easier content, or additional teacher assistance.

🧮 PHASE 9 — First Assessment

After the first instructional condition, administer the assigned parallel assessment.

Primary Outcome: Problem-Solving Performance (PSP)

PSP% = PSP score ÷ 105 × 100

Primary Outcome: Conceptual Understanding (CU)

CU% = CU score ÷ 16 × 100

Supporting Evidence: Spreadsheet–Mathematics Reasoning (SMR)

SMR% = SMR score ÷ 12 × 100
Do not use a combined 133-point score as the primary outcome. PSP and CU answer different research questions.

🔄 PHASE 10 — Teach the Second Condition

Pretest → Condition 1 → Assessment → Condition 2 → Assessment

Counterbalancing reduces the possibility that the observed difference is simply caused by which approach or form came first. Practice/carryover effects can still occur and must be acknowledged as a limitation.

📊 PHASE 12 — Analyze the Actual Data

For PSP and CU separately, calculate:

  • N
  • Mean
  • Median
  • Standard deviation
  • Minimum
  • Maximum

Paired Difference

Dᵢ = Spreadsheetᵢ − Formulaᵢ
  • Paired-samples t-test if assumptions for the paired difference are reasonably met.
  • Wilcoxon signed-rank test if the paired-difference distribution is unsuitable for the paired t-test.

Effect Size

Cohen's dᵧ = mean paired difference ÷ SD of paired differences

For Wilcoxon analysis, use an appropriate matched-pairs rank-based effect-size measure.

Additional Diagnostics

  • Formula-first vs Spreadsheet-first
  • Period 1 vs Period 2
  • Form A vs Form B
  • Treatment fidelity
  • Recorded deviations

🧠 PHASE 13 — Interpret the Findings Carefully

If spreadsheet scores are higher, do not automatically conclude that spreadsheet instruction is superior.

  1. PSP: Did students solve problems better?
  2. CU: Did students demonstrate greater conceptual understanding?
  3. SMR: Could students connect spreadsheet operations to mathematical variables and relationships?
Possible findings include:
  • Higher PSP and CU under spreadsheet instruction
  • Higher PSP with similar CU
  • Higher PSP with lower CU
  • Stronger formula-based performance
  • No meaningful difference
Any of these can be legitimate findings.

Your purpose is not to prove that spreadsheets are better. Your purpose is to determine what happens under the two instructional conditions.

🪞 PHASE 14 — Reflect as a Teacher

1. What did I observe?

Record meaningful classroom observations, learner difficulties, strategies, misconceptions, and implementation issues.

2. What did the data show?

Connect observations to actual PSP, CU, SMR, fidelity, and statistical findings.

3. What did I learn as a teacher?

Examine whether your original assumptions were supported, partly supported, or challenged.

4. What should I change?

Use the evidence to determine the next instructional action.

🚀 PHASE 15 — Evidence-Based Action Plan

The action plan must respond directly to the findings. If learners produce spreadsheet outputs accurately but struggle to explain the mathematics, a future routine might emphasize:

Explain → Model → Spreadsheet → Verify → Interpret

The actual action plan should be based on real findings, not predetermined before results are known.
